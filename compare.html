HOW THE METHODS DIFFER
The serpentine baseline is the standard real-world heuristic — not a strawman. Both pull stock from the nearest location and use the nearest robot; the only differences are batching and stop sequencing.
Batching
OUR: groups orders whose items are physically close (proximity + capacity + priority).
SERPENTINE: takes the next orders FIFO, regardless of where their items live.
Routing
OUR: nearest-neighbour + 2-opt TSP per batch, so the robot rarely backtracks.
SERPENTINE: walks the warehouse snake sequence (S-shape), aisle by aisle. The first pick is automatically the closest.
Pack station
OUR: scored J = dist·0.5 + queue·0.3 + util·0.2 (load-balances docks).
SERPENTINE: nearest dock only.
Why the gap is in multi-order batches: for a single order the snake sequence is already closest-first and near-optimal — our method barely beats it. The gap opens on multi-order batches: serpentine consolidates by arrival (FIFO), so a batch's orders sit anywhere and its snake route sprawls across the warehouse, while our method only batches orders whose items are close and TSP-routes each trip.
